# ph decode on LZ4 residuals vs raw input — M4, 2026-05-17

Quick-look bench probing the "LZ4 + ph" hypothesis (IDEAS.md "LZ4 + ph"
entry, 2026-05-17).  Same data files as the standard MAIN sweep, but
first LZ4-compressed (`lz4 -9 --no-frame-crc`), then byte-frequency-
analyzed and benched as if those bytes were the input.

Methodology: regenerated `bench/dist_real_freqs.h` via
`pivco_file_to_dist` on each LZ4-compressed file, rebuilt the bench,
ran the standard MAIN sweep with `--no-fse`, then restored the
original header.  Per-dataset bench shape unchanged (4M symbols × 25
repeats, min of 5 batches).  Host: M4 Max.

Post-LZ4 byte-entropy is universally in the 7.0-8.0 b/B band — LZ4
flattens histograms (its design).  Datasets that were originally
heavily skewed (calgary_pic 1.21 → 6.63, dna_fasta 2.08 → 7.04) lose
all their proba80-shape.  Already-near-uniform datasets (image_jpeg,
gzip_random) are essentially unchanged.

## Decode speed: ph still wins, but ratios shift

Raw vs LZ4 decode throughput in MB/s, M4 Max, `--no-fse`:

| dataset      | raw `pivco_bu` | LZ4 `pivco_bu` |  Δ%    | raw `huf0_x2` | LZ4 `huf0_x2` |   Δ%  | raw ratio | LZ4 ratio |
|--------------|---------------:|---------------:|-------:|--------------:|--------------:|------:|----------:|----------:|
| html_wiki    |           4535 |           4243 |  -6.4% |          2265 |          1553 | -31%  |     2.00× |     2.66× |
| prose_pride  |           4878 |           3893 | -20.2% |          2498 |          1532 | -39%  |     1.97× |     2.40× |
| image_jpeg   |           4292 |           3934 |  -8.3% |          1366 |          1365 |  0%   |     2.73× |     2.46× |
| json_api     |           4441 |           4311 |  -2.9% |          2388 |          1573 | -34%  |     1.86× |     2.79× |
| gzip_random  |           5234 |           5212 |  -0.4% |             — |             — |   —   |     3.19× |     3.25× |
| chinese_text |           5076 |           4418 | -13.0% |          2061 |          1463 | -29%  |     2.46× |     2.84× |
| calgary_pic  |          11504 |           3945 | -65.7% |          2506 |          1791 | -29%  |     4.64× |     2.20× |

Three observations:

1. **ph's huf0-relative advantage on real text actually *improves* after
   LZ4.**  html_wiki 2.00× → 2.66×, prose_pride 1.97× → 2.40×, json_api
   1.86× → 2.79×, chinese_text 2.46× → 2.84×.  Reason: huf0_x2 drops
   29-39% on the LZ4'd version (its two-symbol-lookup trick depends on
   short codes for frequent bytes — and LZ4 erases that structure by
   flattening); ph drops only 3-13% in the same comparison.
   **Net: ph is the *more* robust Huffman decoder on already-LZ-passed
   data, exactly the regime an LZ4+ph hybrid would face.**

2. **Heavily-skewed datasets lose their ph speedup.**  calgary_pic
   11504 → 3945 (-66%) and image_jpeg slightly negative.  calgary_pic
   went from a proba80-shape (entropy 1.21) where ph's prefill memset +
   skip_node fast paths produce double-digit GB/s, to entropy 6.63 where
   those paths don't fire.  The ph win drops from 4.64× to 2.20× —
   still a win, just a normal one.  Expected: LZ4 eats the very thing
   that made calgary_pic special.

3. **gzip_random and image_jpeg are noise.**  Both were already at or
   near maximum entropy; LZ4 is a near-no-op (97% and 100% of input
   size); decode speeds and ratios essentially unchanged.

## What this means for the LZ4+ph hybrid hypothesis

The positive read: on the *new* regime an LZ-pass pipeline would
expose ph to (post-LZ4 literal streams, byte-entropy ~7.0 b/B,
no extreme skew), **ph still beats huf0_x2 by 2.4-2.8× on real text**.
The huf0_x2 "double-symbol lookup" advantage erodes faster than ph's
SIMD tree-walk does when the input is flattened.  This is the most
favorable possible reading: in a real LZ4 + Huffman codec, the
Huffman stage is exactly this distribution.

The cautious read: absolute throughput drops 5-20% on most real-text
datasets, and 66% on previously-proba80-shape datasets.  ph at
3.9-4.4 GB/s on post-LZ4 text is still fast in absolute terms but
the "10× on flat-tree shapes" headlines from the raw bench don't
transfer.

The honest read: this is a *byte-frequency* probe of an LZ4 residual,
not a true LZ4+Huffman codec.  A real implementation would have to
deal with: per-block dictionary state in LZ4, the offset/length
streams (typically small-alphabet, ph would lose its advantage —
keep on FSE), block-size mismatch (LZ4 wants 64-128 KB, ph wants
4-8 KB), and the cost of the LZ pass itself.  Net throughput would
be dominated by whichever of (LZ4 decompress, ph decode) is slower.
This bench just settles "ph stays competitive on the literal
distribution"; it doesn't yet say "the full pipeline wins."

Trigger to invest further: build a minimal LZ4-residual pipeline
(LZ4 emit + ph encode literals + FSE encode lengths/offsets,
decompose the inverse) and measure end-to-end against zstd at the
same compression ratio.
